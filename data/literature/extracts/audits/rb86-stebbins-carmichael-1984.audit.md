# Stebbins and Carmichael (1984) extraction audit

## Source and machine reads

Primary inputs were `text/rb86-stebbins-carmichael-1984/mineru/` (MinerU Markdown and Table 1/Table 2 HTML blocks) and `text/rb86-stebbins-carmichael-1984/pdftotext-layout.txt`. The MinerU draft converter produced `p002-t01.csv` and `p003-t02.csv`; table QA digit agreement with pdftotext was 0.689 for Table 1 and 0.677 for Table 2. Because those agreements are low and the source OCR layout is unreliable, all table cells in the cell ledger were checked against `pdftoppm` renders: physical PDF pages 2 and 3 at 300 dpi. Equation/prose cells were checked against renders of physical pages 4 and 5. The table row/column layout was checked against each page render once, then every relevant numeric cell and footnote marker was checked individually. Each cell-ledger row records method (b), page, printed table, row, column, the selected reading, and its destination.

The audit contains 60 Table 1 measurement cells across 20 printed runs, four numeric fit-note components, Table 2's 20 analyzed-sample cells (17 numeric and three `n.d.`), two theoretical reference cells, one calculated liquid-composition cell, and located prose/equation pins. Table 1 contributes 8 crystalline and 11 fully liquid observations. Run 229³ (1441 K; 1446.2 J g−1) is the twentieth printed reading, but is partly molten and is retained in context as excluded from the requested crystal/liquid series. Table 2 contains six analyte rows; its C-1/C-2 start/after columns are the per-run sample analyses. Theoretical fayalite is reference context only.

The committed table-tool comparison reports 31 matched points, five mismatches, and two unverifiable points, with no locator errors. The five mismatches are superscripted Table 2 cells where the MinerU draft fuses a marker into the decimal: row 3 column 4 `29.4¹`, row 4 column 4 `70.7¹`, row 6 column 4 `70.7¹`, row 7 column 2 `0.7⁶`, and row 7 column 4 `0¹`. The page 3 render confirms the selected values `29.4`, `70.7`, `70.7`, `0.7`, and `0`; the cell ledger records each render check. The two unverifiable comparison rows are prose fusion results, which do not occur in a draft table.

## Source-reference discrepancy and scope

Table 1's caption explicitly defines `ΔH = H_T − H_300 K`, and the Calorimetry section gives a 300 K reference. The task wording says 400 K; the extract follows the paper and preserves the 300 K relative increment. No value is promoted to absolute enthalpy. The nearest quantity token, `H_minus_H298`, specifically means H(T)−H(298.15 K) under `simulator/battery/migrate.py::_H_MINUS_H298_ANCHOR` and `simulator/battery/enums.py::Quantity.H_MINUS_H298`; there is no exact 300 K token in `simulator/battery/migrate.py::QUANTITY_ALIASES`. The 19 Table 1 readings therefore retain a descriptive non-token quantity and remain typed refusals with values and units intact. Their printed T_K is still bound to Identity and retained as a located point condition; no 298.15 K subtype is assigned. The migrator's `simulator/battery/migrate.py::_convert_compilation_amount` accepts molar units for this path, while these data are J g−1 and the liquid sample has no safe row-specific molar basis.

No separate liquid Cp is printed. The Table 1 fit note is `ΔH = 1.1960 T − 255.86 J g−1` for runs 210–220, with `σ_H = 9.2 J g−1` and `σ_Cp = 0.045 J g−1 K−1`; it has no stated temperature interval. Equation (6) prints `ΔH_T^liq = −295.23 + 1.1960 T J per g sample`, without a temperature interval. Both coefficient bundles remain typed refusals; the slope was not differentiated into a Cp. The authors' fusion functions in Eqs. (10) and (11), including the printed 900–1900 K interval for Eq. (11), are preserved in context.

## Table 2 schema and evidence decisions

Table 2 whole-sample analyses are 17 numeric values and three `n.d.` cells, all stored at printed percent with canonical unit `%` and typed refusal. The code's `OBSERVATION_TYPES` allows `concentration_series`, but `Quantity` has no exact generic quantity for a multiphase whole-sample chemical analysis. `equilibrium_phase_composition` is a near miss because Table 2 is not one defined phase. The schema gap cites `tools/validate_literature_extracts.py::OBSERVATION_TYPES`, `simulator/battery/enums.py::Quantity`, `simulator/battery/enums.py::Quantity.EQUILIBRIUM_PHASE_COMPOSITION`, `simulator/battery/migrate.py::QUANTITY_ALIASES`, `simulator/battery/migrate.py::TYPE_QUANTITY`, `simulator/battery/enums.py::PHASE_BASIS_TOKENS`, and `simulator/battery/enums.py::PhaseRole`.

FeOtot is the total-iron assay reported as FeO. The C-2 starting-material FeO 70.7¹ and Fe2O3 0¹ are the microprobe values identified by footnote 1. C-2 post-calorimetry FeO 62.3 has no such marker: page 293 describes the colorimetric and titrimetric chemical ferrous-iron assays, calibrated for metallic Fe, and states that the total-iron assay plus both ferrous-iron assays yield the true ferrous, ferric, and metallic contents. The individual assay readings are not tabulated; 62.3 wt.% is the published reduced composition. C-2 post-run Fe2O3 6.5 is footnote 5's FeO/FeOtot result, with its lineage including printed FeO 62.3, FeOtot 70.1 wt.% as FeO, and metallic Fe 1.5 wt.%; the rounded inputs give about 6.52 wt.% Fe2O3, consistent with the printed 6.5. Fe° starting zeros are determined from absence of magnetic material. The source prints precision of ±0.5% for total iron, ±20% for Fe2O3, and ±50% for Fe°; confidence level is not stated, and no cell-specific uncertainty is manufactured.

The Table 2 `Fe2O3(liq)` 2.1 wt.% cell is a measured-reduced composition for the liquid in equilibrium with metal at 1520 K, footnote 7. C-1 pre-quench liquid Fe2O3 is about 3.7±1 wt.% from its iron-loss measurements; the spatial capsule-wall inputs and integration are not tabulated, so the derivation records located `raw_inputs` as `not_printed`. The original Table 2 bulk analysis is of quenched solids, not glass; no glass-phase observation is created.

## Corrections, conditions, and erratum

C-1 capsule-wall analysis reports at least 15% Fe at silicate contact, near zero by 0.1 mm into platinum, requiring 1–2% iron loss as FeO. C-2 plating remained intact and the text estimates about 0.5±0.5% FeO loss; the quenched bulk sample contains about 1.5% metallic Fe. The authors use Eq. (3) to correct quench disproportionation, infer about 2 wt.% Fe2O3 in the equilibrium peritectic liquid, and report the 0–2 wt.% range from iron-loss data alone. Cp corrections of 0.18% to the equilibrium liquid and 0.34% to pure fayalite liquid were ignored against an experimental Cp error of about 3.5%.

The 88.4±1.1 kJ mol−1 result is explicitly at 1 bar and 1490 K, and its located point condition retains 100000 Pa. Its printed reaction produces a multicomponent peritectic liquid plus Fe metal, so it is typed-refused with quantity unknown and value unavailable. The existing `Quantity.ENTHALPY_OF_FUSION` path through `simulator/battery/generators/janaf.py::janaf_fusion_enthalpy` represents pure same-formula solid-to-liquid fusion; it cannot identify this reaction. The 89.3±1.1 kJ mol−1 hypothetical congruent value is the pure Fe2SiO4 solid-to-liquid fusion row and remains admitted as `heat_of_fusion`; no temperature or total pressure is explicitly attached to that value. Run-level total pressure, fO2, and full high-temperature liquid composition are not printed and remain unknown; argon filling is recorded as capsule detail only. No 1 bar condition is copied to calorimetry rows.

The Am. Mineral. 69:1198 erratum corrects only the laihunite reference, to G. Y. Chao (1978), American Mineralogist 63, 424–425. It makes no change to data or equations. The inherited Eq. (8) citation is Robie, Finch, and Hemingway (1982). Figure 1's extra plotted points remain a figure-read candidate and were not transcribed.

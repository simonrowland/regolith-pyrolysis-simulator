## Pressures and evaporation coefficients

The text says partial pressures of PO, PO2, P2, Zn, Na and NaPO3 were obtained from the Hertz–Knudsen equation using evaporation coefficients from [14] and [15]. No numerical pressure or evaporation coefficient is printed. Nothing was invented.

## Standard state

`reference_state_from_extract` was called on the three prose strings.

The published page 61 sentence, checked on the 200 dpi render of PDF page 4, is: "Taking the pure oxides which form the ternary system as the standard states the Gibbs-Duhem equation could be written as". That clause is quoted in every admitted activity `standard_state`. It does not name solid or liquid by itself. The preceding sentence on the same page says "liquid phase" of the melt. That word was left out of the ZnO and Na2O strings so the mapper does not see two phases.

ZnO. Reaction (1) is printed [ZnO] = (Zn) + (O), and the paper says square brackets indicate the solid. Prose ends "pure solid ZnO". Migration returns Raoultian crystalline ZnO. The phase is the one the paper names. It is not an inference. 1100 K is far below the ZnO melting region; the paper does not print a ZnO melting temperature, and no melting-band deferral was applied.

Na2O. Equation (10) is printed [Na2O] = 2(Na) + (O), same bracket convention. Prose ends "pure solid Na2O". Migration returns Raoultian crystalline Na2O. Same ruling: the paper names the solid. The paper does not print a Na2O melting temperature. 1100 K is not inside about 100 K of the Na2O melting point.

P2O5. The page 61 sentence names the pure oxides as the standard states. Equation (14)'s a* at x* is the lower limit of the Gibbs-Duhem integral (16.6 ± 0.2 or 56.0 ± 0.2 mol% ZnO). It is not a different standard state. Equations (12) and (13) are built on reactions (2) and (11), which are written with solid [P4O10]. The plotted component is P2O5. The prose ends "The activity reference phase is not stated." Migration returns unknown. Stable-phase-at-T was not applied: the paper does not state a pure-P2O5 reference phase, and the bracketed solid is a different formula. The x* anchor stays in context and is not a new field.

Sample phase on the activity rows is liquid because the paper calls the activities melt activities at 1100 K. That phase is the melt, not the reference.

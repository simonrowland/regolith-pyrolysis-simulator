## Duplicate check

Not a duplicate. The next article in the same issue is `shornikov-2003-mullite-thermodynamics`, RJPC 77(7) 1044–1050, “Thermodynamic Properties of Mullite.” Different title, different pages. References [1] and [2] of this paper are the 2000 vapourization paper (Zh. Fiz. Khim. 74(5), p. 775) and the 2000 melts paper (p. 783). This article does not print 12.9 ± 0.8 kJ/mol or 11.6 ± 0.4 J/(mol K). Those digits are in the 2002 Doklady paper and in the mullite compilation. They are not copied here.

The 2002 Doklady paper is a sibling report of the same solid-phase campaign (same eight compositions, same transition bounds, same 60 ± 5 mol% mullite). It is a different article. Shared numbers are cross-noted, not treated as a duplicate.

## Context rows

Mullite 60 ± 5 mol% Al2O3, equation (17), published page 1042 (PDF page 6). Same result as the Doklady paper.

Phase-transition estimates, the same bounds as the Doklady paper: reactions (12) and (13) did not exceed 5 kJ/mol, stated on published page 1039; reaction (14) is 13 ± 10 kJ/mol on published page 1041. Cited transition temperatures 1857 K and 2123 K are from [3], not measured here.

ΔfusH(Al2O3) = 105 ± 25 kJ/mol, “found in this work,” published page 1041. It is the difference of reaction-(3) enthalpies below and above the melting point. The melting point 2327 K is cited from [49]. The paper says the estimate is less accurate than 111.4 ± 3.0 kJ/mol [54]. Kept as context: an author estimate whose temperature is cited, not opened as a scored heat_of_fusion identity. The 2026-10-20 pure-oxide rule does not apply. This is a fusion enthalpy, not an activity reference, and the paper does not present it as an ion current over pure alumina at the experiment temperature.

At 1857 K, p*(Al), p*(AlO), and p*(O) do not exceed 10^−9 atm. p*(O) deviates from p°(O) over silica by not more than 10%. Bounds, not points.

Evaporation coefficients were assumed equal to the simple oxides via Komlev. Not measured. Not alpha.
